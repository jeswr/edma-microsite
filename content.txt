ABOUT THE COMMUNITY
=====================================================================

Heading: A two-way exchange between industry and the Solid project

Intro:
The Community of Practice pairs the data-management expertise of the EDM
Association's members and partners with Solid's open, standards-based
approach to giving people and organisations genuine control over their data.
Industry brings the requirements, domain models and demand of regulated
sectors; Solid brings a way to break down the silos, lock-in and duplication
that hold those sectors back.

[Card 1] The EDM Association
The global association elevating data management as a strategic business
priority. EDMA convenes and sponsors the community, bringing its member and
partner network, the FIBO ontology, the CDMC framework, the EDMConnect
platform, and the authority to engage regulators, standards bodies and
commercial partners.

[Card 2] The Open Data Institute
The ODI became Solid's steward in October 2024, bringing more than a decade
of experience in responsible data innovation. Its stewardship ensures the
infrastructure develops to benefit society, with governance that reflects
community values and standards that enable global interoperability. The ODI
maintains Solid's open-source foundations, is taking the Linked Web Storage
specification through the W3C, trains hundreds of new developers, and
catalyses use-case pilots that demonstrate real impact.

[Panel] What is Solid?
Solid is a privacy-by-design protocol built on standard web technologies.
Rather than each application holding its own copy of a person's or
organisation's data, Solid stores it in a Pod: a personal or organisational
datastore with fine-grained access controls, so applications read and write
only with permission. Because Pods use open, linked-data standards, the same
data can be reused across applications without bespoke integration, and it
stays under the control of the entity it describes. Solid was developed by
Sir Tim Berners-Lee's research group at MIT, nurtured by a global community,
and is standardised through the W3C Solid Community Group and the Linked Web
Storage Working Group.

WHY SOLID [dark-blue section]
=====================================================================

Heading: The problem is structural: every service keeps its own copy of your data

Intro:
The same data is collected, copied and secured many times over, yet is rarely
current, portable or under the control of the person or organisation it
describes. The result: silos and duplication, concentrated breach risk,
vendor lock-in, coarse consent, and identity re-proofed at every institution.
Solid fixes this at the root by decoupling data from applications.

[Eight benefit cards]

1. Reduced data custody and breach exposure
   Organisations read data from the Pod instead of holding and securing their
   own copies, lowering cost, liability and compliance burden.

2. Reusable verifiable credentials
   Already-proven identity and eligibility attributes are reused across
   sectors, cutting onboarding time and KYC/AML cost.

3. A single, current source of truth
   Less reconciliation effort and stale-copy error; customers and citizens
   update their details once, not everywhere.

4. Interoperability without lock-in
   An open W3C-track standard; semantic interoperability comes from the
   shared sector ontologies this group will define.

5. New permissioned services and revenue
   Value-added services, such as bank-provided identity, built on
   user-controlled data.

6. AI- and agent-ready data
   As AI agents develop, Solid keeps users in control: structured, consented,
   attributable data as a foundation for trustworthy AI.

7. Regulatory future-proofing
   Demonstrable user control aligns with GDPR Article 20, the EU Data Act
   and the Digital Markets Act.

8. Better customer experience
   Data that travels with people across platforms and providers, for more
   trustworthy digital experiences.

Closing note:
Real implementations prove the model works: the Flanders/athumi citizen-data
ecosystem, VITO's healthcare platform, Graphmetrix in US construction supply
chains, and pilots with the BBC, the Bezos Earth Fund, NatWest Bank and Visa
across media, sustainability, banking and commerce. Adoption is still early;
assessing that readiness and closing the gaps is the remit of this community.

SECTORS & USE CASES
=====================================================================

Heading: Proving the value where the stakes are highest

Intro:
Finance, healthcare and government are the priorities because that is where
the costs of the status quo are highest and where credible champions already
sit. Demonstrating value there is the fastest route to wider adoption.

[FINANCE column]
Tagline: Home of the EDM Association's roots and the FIBO community, and the
first sector the community is proving Solid in.

• Lending on verified, current data
  Loan and mortgage agreements modelled in FIBO and verified against the
  borrower's Pod let lenders read data at source instead of re-keyed,
  hard-to-verify copies.

• Reusable KYC/AML onboarding
  Verifiable credentials for identity, address and source of funds are
  proofed once and confirmed by signature check, cutting weeks of onboarding
  and significant compliance cost per client.

• Bank-provided identity services
  Banks issue credentials into a customer's Pod and let the customer present
  them to third parties, turning a compliance cost centre into a service
  line.

[HEALTHCARE column]
Tagline: Among the most mature areas of Solid deployment, with real clinical
data and real consent already in production pilots.

• Patient-controlled records
  VITO's WeAre vaults in Flanders let patients share health data across
  providers and researchers; an NHS pilot in Greater Manchester used personal
  stores to improve dementia care.

• Medication management
  A medication record in the patient's Pod, described with shared,
  standardised definitions, gives pharmacists, prescribers and hospitals one
  current view and fewer reconciliation errors.

• A governed home for consumer health data
  Readings from consumer devices that fall outside HIPAA gain a consented,
  governed home, addressing a gap regulators are actively grappling with.

[GOVERNMENT column]
Tagline: The clearest evidence yet that citizen-controlled data can operate
as public infrastructure.

• Citizen vaults and "tell us once"
  Flanders' data utility athumi, built on Solid, is architected to serve
  ~6.5 million citizens, with live services including diploma sharing and
  online death registration.

• Social services for vulnerable citizens
  A citizen-controlled Pod holds verified documents and releases only what
  each service needs: control and dignity for the data subject. Code for
  PDX's Solid wallet already helps people at risk of homelessness complete
  housing-assistance applications.

• Less repeated admin
  UK residents spend roughly 56 hours a year on government admin. A
  government-backed vault lets citizens maintain core data once, with
  services reading it by permission.

Closing note:
See the Solid Project website for deployed services and applications, and
the ODI's visual walkthrough of the Solid user experience.

WORK PROGRAMME
=====================================================================

Heading: What the community will deliver

Intro:
The deliverables follow directly from the community's objectives: raise
awareness and adoption, assess Solid's readiness, demonstrate value through
real deployments, and develop the best practice and commercial understanding
that adoption depends on.

[Six numbered cards]

1. Use cases & reference implementations
   Demonstrable deployments showing Solid's value in each sector, with white
   papers and webinars detailing implementers' experience.

2. Sector ontology bindings
   Recommendations for how established domain ontologies, identifier schemes
   and terminologies (FIBO and peers) bind to Solid in each sector, rather
   than inventing new ontologies.

3. Security & functional requirements
   What a Solid service must do to be deployed in a given sector, including
   how providers satisfy cloud and data-management expectations such as CDMC.

4. The Solid Certification
   A neutral, evidence-based mark certifying that a Solid server, application
   or identity provider meets a target sector's bar. It layers a recognised
   cyber-security baseline, CDMC data-control conformance and sector-specific
   requirements, with assurance tiered from self-assessment to independent
   verification.

5. Solid–DPROD bindings for data products
   Recommendations for how DPROD (the EDM Council/EKGF profile of W3C DCAT)
   describes where the data products in a Pod live and how to request access.

6. An open community platform
   A neutral home for the community, branded with the Solid identity and
   hosted on EDMConnect, with every output published under the MIT licence.

Heading: Open participation, consensus decisions

Intro:
The community starts as an open forum while the work is defined, then
converts into a formal working group with defined work items and
deliverables. Decisions are taken by consensus, facilitated by the co-chairs,
with the EDM Association and the Open Data Institute providing overall
stewardship.
