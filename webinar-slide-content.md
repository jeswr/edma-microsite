## Slide 7 — What is Solid

**Layout:** Two columns — text left, Pod diagram + Solid logo right.

- **Heading:** What is Solid
- **Body:**
  - Solid is an open-source **project**, open **standard**, and **movement** for a **user-centric data** storage system, originating from Sir Tim Berners-Lee's lab at MIT CSAIL.
  - Your data lives in a **Pod**: a personal or organisational datastore with fine-grained access controls. Apps read and write only **with permission**, **breaking down data silos** and keeping data under the control of whoever it describes.
  - The ODI stewards Solid's standards, codebases, and communities.
- **Imagery:** "Solid Pods" network diagram + Solid logo.

**Notes (Jesse, ~1.5 min):** Solid is an open standard for user-centric data and identity on the web; data lives in a Pod, apps read/write only with permission, always under the control of whoever it describes.

## Slide 8 — The problem

**Layout:** Two columns — list left, cartoon figure right.

- **Heading:** Every service keeps its own copy of your data
- **Body:** Collected, copied and secured many times over, yet rarely current, portable or under your control:
  - Silos and duplication
  - Concentrated breach risk
  - Vendor lock-in
  - Coarse, all-or-nothing consent
  - Identity re-proofed at every institution
- **Closing line (bold):** Solid fixes this at the root by decoupling data from applications.
- **Imagery:** "Walled Gardens" cartoon, by David Simonds (with caption).

**Notes (Jesse, ~1.5 min):** The same five problems in every sector; Solid fixes all five by decoupling data from applications — read from the Pod with permission instead of securing another copy.

## Slide 10 — The standard / protocol stack

**Layout:** Full-width diagram of the web protocol stack with Solid as the top layer.

- **Heading:** What is Solid (client-server specs)
- **Diagram content:** Bottom-up stack — physical (Ethernet, WiFi, Mobile data, Fibre) → IP v4/v6 → TCP → DNS → URI → HTTP → HTML → JS/ECMAScript → Web APIs → **Solid layer: Universal Data API (LDP) · AuthN (global SSO) · AuthZ**. Era markers: Web 1.0 (HTTP/HTML/URL), Web 2.0 (JavaScript/Web APIs), Web 3.0 (Global SSO, AuthZ, Universal API).

**Notes (Jesse, ~1 min):** Solid is the next web layer, adding the three things the web never standardised: global SSO, fine-grained authorization, and a universal data API. All open W3C standards. "Let's show you."

## Slide 11 — Live demo

**Layout:** "Live demo" flag; numbered steps.

- **Flag:** Live demo
- **Heading:** One Pod, two apps, real-time consent
- **Steps:**
  1. Log in once with a WebID: global single sign-on
  2. App A writes a profile record into the Pod
  3. App B reads the same data: no re-entry, no integration
  4. Grant, then revoke, access to a single resource
- **Footer:** Driven & narrated live by Jesse Wright

## Slide 16 — What we're building

**Layout:** Lead paragraph + three coloured-tick blocks.

- **Kicker:** Jim Halcomb & Elisa Kendall
- **Heading:** Putting one copy of the truth into practice
- **Lead:** A **community of practice** is a neutral, vendor-independent group where finance, healthcare and government work alongside the people building Solid to make it production-ready for regulated data. Built on 300+ participants in the W3C Solid Community Group; every output MIT-licensed.
- **Blocks:**
  - (green) **Real pilots per sector** — Demonstrable deployments in finance, healthcare and government — not slideware.
  - (teal) **Aligned to sector standards** — How Solid binds to the models these industries already rely on — starting with FIBO in finance.
  - (orange) **Independent certification** — A trusted, neutral way to certify that a Solid service meets a sector's requirements.

**Notes (Jim & Elisa, ~3 min):** Jim frames what a CoP is and the three deliverables (real pilots; alignment to sector standards; independent certification); Elisa adds the FIBO point for finance. Everything MIT-licensed and open to all.
