What is Solid?

An open standard for user-centric data and identity on the web

Instead of every application holding its own copy of your data, Solid stores it in a Pod — a personal or organisational datastore with fine-grained access controls. Applications read and write only with permission.

Because Pods use open, linked-data standards, the same data is reused across apps without bespoke integration — and it stays under the control of whoever it describes.

Privacy by design

Fine-grained, revocable access control on every resource.

Built on web standards

From Sir Tim Berners-Lee's group at MIT; standardised at the W3C.

Stewarded by the ODI

Deployed across government, finance and healthcare.

SOLIDsolidproject.org
JESSE — (≈1.5 min): "Thanks Louise. I'm Jesse Wright, and I'm here with Matthieu Bosquet — we both work on Solid at the ODI. Let me give you the one-sentence version: Solid is an open standard for user-centric data and identity on the web. Here's the shift. Today, every app you use keeps its own copy of your data. Solid inverts that. Your data lives in a Pod — think of it as a personal or organisational datastore — and applications read and write to it only with your permission, at the level of individual pieces of data. Because Pods use open, linked-data standards, the same data can be reused across many applications without custom integration, and it always stays under the control of whoever it describes. It's privacy by design, it's built entirely on standard web technology from Tim's group at MIT, and it's now stewarded by the ODI and standardised at the W3C."

The problem Solid solves

Every service keeps its own copy of your data

The same data is collected, copied and secured many times over — yet rarely current, portable or under your control.

  • Silos and duplication
  • Concentrated breach risk
  • Vendor lock-in
  • Coarse, all-or-nothing consent
  • Identity re-proofed at every institution

Solid fixes this at the root by decoupling data from applications.

Read data from the Pod instead of holding your own copy. Lower cost, lower liability, and a single current source of truth.

SOLIDsolidproject.org
MATTHIEU — (≈1.5 min): "I'm Matthieu. Let me make the problem concrete, because it's the same five problems in every sector. One: silos and duplication — the same fact stored in dozens of places. Two: breach risk concentrates, because every copy is a target. Three: vendor lock-in, because your data is trapped in someone's product. Four: consent is coarse — it's all-or-nothing, take-it-or-leave-it. And five: your identity gets re-proven from scratch at every single institution you deal with. Solid fixes all five at the root, by decoupling the data from the applications. The organisation reads the data it needs from the Pod, with permission, instead of holding and securing yet another copy. That means lower cost, lower liability, and — for the first time — a single, current source of truth that the data subject actually controls."

The standard

The next layer of the web

Web 1.0

HTTP · HTML · URLs — the document web.

Web 2.0

JavaScript & Web APIs — the interactive web.

Web 3.0 · Solid

Global single sign-on, authorization, and a universal data API (LDP).

Solid adds three missing pieces to the open web stack: global SSO (one identity), fine-grained authorization, and a universal read/write data API — all on open W3C standards, no lock-in.

SOLIDsolidproject.org
MATTHIEU — (≈1 min): "One slide on where this sits technically. Web 1.0 gave us HTTP, HTML and URLs — the document web. Web 2.0 added JavaScript and web APIs — the interactive web. Solid is the next layer: it adds the three things the web never standardised — a global single sign-on so you have one identity everywhere, fine-grained authorization so you control access per-resource, and a universal data API so any app can read and write your data in a standard way. Crucially, all of this is open, W3C-track standards. There's no proprietary chain, no lock-in. Now — rather than describe it, let's show you."

Elisa Kendall · FIBO & financial services

FIBO is the bridge between finance and Solid

Finance already has a shared, rigorous vocabulary in FIBO. Binding FIBO to Solid means data in a Pod means the same thing to every institution — semantic interoperability without inventing anything new.

We bind established ontologies, identifier schemes and terminologies to Solid — we don't reinvent them. That's the fastest, safest route to adoption in a regulated sector.

Lending on verified, current data

Agreements modelled in FIBO, verified against the borrower's Pod — read at source, not re-keyed.

Reusable KYC/AML

Identity, address and source-of-funds proofed once, confirmed by signature check — weeks and cost out of onboarding.

Bank-provided identity

Banks issue credentials into a Pod the customer presents elsewhere — a compliance cost centre becomes a service line. (e.g. NatWest's Solid pilots.)

ODI × EDM ASSOCIATIONSolid Community of Practice
ELISA — (≈5 min): "Thank you, Roberto. I'm Elisa Kendall — I've spent much of my career on ontologies, and I co-lead the FIBO and ontology work here. I want to explain why FIBO is the bridge that makes this real for financial services, and why finance is the right first sector. FIBO — the Financial Industry Business Ontology — is the result of years of work by the financial community to agree, precisely and machine-readably, what financial terms mean: what a loan is, what a legal entity is, what a counterparty is. The problem in finance has never been a shortage of data; it's that the same concept means subtly different things in every system, so data can't move without expensive, error-prone reconciliation. Now connect that to Solid. Solid gives you the place to put data under the data subject's control, and a standard way to read and write it. But control without shared meaning isn't enough — two banks reading the same Pod still need to agree on what the fields mean. FIBO supplies exactly that shared meaning. So binding FIBO to Solid gives you semantic interoperability: data in a Pod that means the same thing to every institution that reads it. And critically, we are binding an ontology finance already trusts — we are not asking anyone to adopt something new. Let me make it concrete with three use cases. First, lending: imagine a loan or mortgage agreement modelled in FIBO and verified against the borrower's own Pod. The lender reads current, verified data at source, instead of working from re-keyed, hard-to-verify copies. Second, KYC and AML onboarding: identity, address and source-of-funds verified once and held as verifiable credentials in the Pod; the next institution confirms them with a signature check, taking weeks and serious cost out of onboarding. Third, bank-provided identity: a bank issues credentials into the customer's Pod that the customer then presents to third parties — turning what is today a pure compliance cost centre into a new service line. NatWest has already piloted exactly this kind of customer-controlled flow on Solid. This is why finance leads: the EDM Association's roots are here, FIBO is here, and the cost of the status quo is enormous. Prove it in finance and the path to healthcare and government opens up. Stuart will take that to the bottom line."

Stuart Coleman · business value

The business case: cost out, risk down, new revenue in

Less data custody

Read from the Pod instead of holding copies — lower cost, liability and compliance burden.

Reusable credentials

Proven identity & eligibility reused across sectors — onboarding and KYC/AML cost falls.

Single source of truth

Update once, not everywhere — less reconciliation, fewer stale-copy errors.

Interoperability, no lock-in

Open W3C-track standard; shared sector ontologies, not bespoke integrations.

STUART — (≈5 min): "Thanks, Elisa. I'm Stuart Coleman. Elisa's given you the how; I want to give you the why-it-pays, because this only scales if it makes commercial sense — and it does, in six ways. First, you carry less data custody. Today every organisation holds and secures its own copy of customer data; with Solid you read what you need from the Pod, with permission. That's directly lower storage cost, lower liability, and a lighter compliance burden — every copy you don't hold is a breach you can't have. Second, reusable credentials: identity and eligibility attributes that are proven once and reused across services and even across sectors. That takes real time and real money out of onboarding and KYC/AML. Third, a single, current source of truth. When the customer updates their details once — in their Pod — rather than in twenty places, you spend far less on reconciliation and you make fewer decisions on stale data. Fourth, interoperability without lock-in: this is an open, W3C-track standard, and the interoperability comes from shared sector ontologies this community defines — not from yet another bespoke integration project or a proprietary platform you can never leave. Fifth — and this is the one that turns the conversation from cost to growth — new permissioned services and revenue. Once data is user-controlled, you can offer genuinely new value-added services on top of it; bank-provided identity is the obvious example, turning a cost centre into a product. And sixth, you future-proof: Solid gives you data that's structured, consented and attributable — the right foundation for trustworthy AI and AI agents — and demonstrable user control that aligns you with GDPR Article 20, the EU Data Act and the Digital Markets Act. You get ahead of regulation instead of scrambling after it. So the business case is simple: cost out, risk down, and new revenue in — while doing right by your customers. The way to capture that is to be in the room while the rails are being laid. Let me hand back to tell you how."
