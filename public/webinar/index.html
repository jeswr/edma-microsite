Introduction to Solid

What it is, the problem it solves, and a live demo

Jesse Wright · Project Lead – Solid, the ODI · 10 minutes

Jesse Wright
Project Lead – Solid, the ODI

What is Solid

Solid is an open-source project, open standard, and movement for a user-centric data storage system, originating from Sir Tim Berners-Lee's lab at MIT CSAIL.

Your data lives in a Pod: a personal or organisational datastore with fine-grained access controls. Apps read and write only with permission, breaking down data silos and keeping data under the control of whoever it describes.

The ODI stewards Solid's standards, codebases, and communities.

JESSE (~1.5 min): "I'm Jesse Wright, Solid Project Lead at the ODI. One sentence: Solid is an open standard for user-centric data and identity on the web. Today every app keeps its own copy of your data; Solid inverts that. Your data lives in a Pod, and apps — and, as you'll see, AI agents — read and write only with your permission, at the level of individual items. Open linked-data standards mean the same data is reused everywhere without custom integration, always under the control of whoever it describes. Privacy by design, built on standard web tech from Tim's group at MIT, stewarded by the ODI, standardised at the W3C."

Every service keeps its own copy of your data

Collected, copied and secured many times over, yet rarely current, portable or under your control:

  • Silos and duplication
  • Concentrated breach risk
  • Vendor lock-in
  • Coarse, all-or-nothing consent
  • Identity re-proofed at every institution

Solid fixes this at the root by decoupling data from applications.

Walled Gardens, by David Simonds

JESSE (~1.5 min): "It's the same five problems in every sector: silos and duplication; breach risk that concentrates because every copy is a target; vendor lock-in; coarse, all-or-nothing consent; and identity re-proven from scratch at every institution. Solid fixes all five at the root by decoupling the data from the applications — the organisation reads what it needs from the Pod, with permission, instead of securing yet another copy. Lower cost, lower liability, and a single current source of truth the data subject controls."

The missing data layer for trustworthy AI

Data sovereignty

People and organisations keep genuine control of their data as AI proliferates.

Consented and attributable

Structured, permissioned, attributable data: the right base for AI you can trust.

Reusable across agents

An agent reads once, with consent, instead of each service scraping its own copy.

As AI agents act on our behalf, the urgent question is what data they act on, and who controls it.

JESSE (~1.5 min): "Why now? Because of AI. As agents act on our behalf — booking, applying, negotiating — the urgent question is what data they act on, and who controls it. If it's a scraped, stale copy in someone's silo, that's neither safe nor trustworthy. Solid gives agents structured, consented, attributable data from a source the user controls — the agent reads what it needs, with permission, instead of every service hoarding its own copy. Solid is arguably the missing data layer for the agentic era. That's a big part of why this community forms now."

What is Solid (client-server specs)

JESSE (~1 min): "Where this sits technically: Web 1.0 gave us HTTP, HTML and URLs — the document web. Web 2.0 added JavaScript and web APIs — the interactive web. Solid is the next layer, adding the three things the web never standardised: a global single sign-on so you have one identity everywhere, fine-grained authorization so you control access per resource, and a universal data API so any app can read and write your data in a standard way. All open W3C standards — no lock-in. Rather than describe it, let's show you."
Live demo

One Pod, two apps, real-time consent

  2. App A writes a profile record into the Pod
  3. App B reads the same data: no re-entry, no integration
  4. Grant, then revoke, access to a single resource

Putting one copy of the truth into practice

A community of practice is a neutral, vendor-independent group where finance, healthcare and government work alongside the people building Solid to make it production-ready for regulated data. Built on 300+ participants in the W3C Solid Community Group; every output MIT-licensed.

Real pilots per sector

Demonstrable deployments in finance, healthcare and government — not slideware.

Aligned to sector standards

How Solid binds to the models these industries already rely on — starting with FIBO in finance.

Independent certification

A trusted, neutral way to certify that a Solid service meets a sector's requirements.

JIM & ELISA (~3 min): Jim — "A community of practice is simply a neutral space where the people with the problem and the people building the technology solve it together. Ours brings the EDM Association's members — the data-management leaders of finance, healthcare and government — together with the Solid community. We do three concrete things. One: run real pilots in each sector, so this is proven, not promised. Two: agree how Solid aligns with the data standards these industries already rely on —" Elisa: "— and in finance that means FIBO, the Financial Industry Business Ontology, so a Pod's data means the same thing to every institution." Jim — "Three: build a trusted, independent way to certify that a Solid service meets a sector's requirements. Everything is MIT-licensed and open to all."
