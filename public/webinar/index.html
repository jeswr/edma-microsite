What is Solid?

An open standard for user-centric data and identity

Instead of every app holding its own copy of your data, Solid stores it in a Pod, a personal or organisational datastore with fine-grained access controls. Apps read and write only with permission.

Open, linked-data standards mean the same data is reused across apps without bespoke integration, and stays under the control of whoever it describes.

SOLIDsolidproject.org
JESSE (~1.5 min): "I'm Jesse Wright, here with Matthieu Bosquet — we both work on Solid at the ODI. One sentence: Solid is an open standard for user-centric data and identity on the web. Today every app keeps its own copy of your data; Solid inverts that. Your data lives in a Pod, and apps — and, as you'll see, AI agents — read and write only with your permission, at the level of individual items. Open linked-data standards mean the same data is reused everywhere without custom integration, always under the control of whoever it describes. Privacy by design, built on standard web tech from Tim's group at MIT, stewarded by the ODI, standardised at the W3C."

The problem Solid solves

Every service keeps its own copy of your data

Collected, copied and secured many times over, yet rarely current, portable or under your control.

  • Silos and duplication
  • Concentrated breach risk
  • Vendor lock-in
  • Coarse, all-or-nothing consent
  • Identity re-proofed at every institution

Solid fixes this at the root by decoupling data from applications.

“Walled Gardens”, illustration by David Simonds

SOLIDsolidproject.org
MATTHIEU (~1.5 min): "I'm Matthieu. It's the same five problems in every sector: silos and duplication; breach risk that concentrates because every copy is a target; vendor lock-in; coarse, all-or-nothing consent; and identity re-proven from scratch at every institution. Solid fixes all five at the root by decoupling the data from the applications — the organisation reads what it needs from the Pod, with permission, instead of securing yet another copy. Lower cost, lower liability, and a single current source of truth the data subject controls."

Consented & attributable

Reusable across agents

SOLIDsolidproject.org
JESSE (~1.5 min): "Why now? Because of AI. As agents act on our behalf — booking, applying, negotiating — the urgent question is what data they act on, and who controls it. If it's a scraped, stale copy in someone's silo, that's neither safe nor trustworthy. Solid gives agents structured, consented, attributable data from a source the user controls — the agent reads what it needs, with permission, instead of every service hoarding its own copy. Solid is arguably the missing data layer for the agentic era. That's a big part of why this community forms now."

The standard

The next layer of the open web

Solid adds the three pieces the web never standardised:

  • Global single sign-on, one identity, everywhere
  • Fine-grained authorization, access controlled per resource
  • A universal data API, any app reads & writes in a standard way

All open, W3C-track standards. No proprietary chain, no lock-in.

SOLIDsolidproject.org
MATTHIEU (~1 min): "Where this sits technically: Web 1.0 gave us HTTP, HTML and URLs — the document web. Web 2.0 added JavaScript and web APIs — the interactive web. Solid is the next layer, adding the three things the web never standardised: a global single sign-on so you have one identity everywhere, fine-grained authorization so you control access per resource, and a universal data API so any app can read and write your data in a standard way. All open W3C standards — no lock-in. Rather than describe it, let's show you."

Elisa Kendall · FIBO & financial services

FIBO is the bridge between finance and Solid

Finance already has a shared, rigorous vocabulary in FIBO. Bind it to Solid and data in a Pod means the same thing to every institution, semantic interoperability, nothing new invented.

Lending on verified, current data

Agreements modelled in FIBO, verified against the borrower's Pod, read at source, not re-keyed.

Reusable KYC / AML

Identity, address and source-of-funds proofed once, confirmed by signature check, weeks and cost out of onboarding.

Bank-provided identity

Banks issue credentials into a Pod the customer presents elsewhere, a cost centre becomes a service line. (e.g. NatWest's Solid pilots.)

ODI × EDM ASSOCIATIONSolid Community of Practice
ELISA (~5 min): "I'm Elisa Kendall; I co-lead the FIBO and ontology work. FIBO — the Financial Industry Business Ontology — is years of work by the financial community to agree, machine-readably, what terms mean: a loan, a legal entity, a counterparty. Finance's problem isn't a shortage of data; it's that the same concept means subtly different things in every system, so data can't move without expensive reconciliation. Solid gives you the place to put data under the subject's control and a standard way to read it — but control without shared meaning isn't enough; two banks reading the same Pod still need to agree what the fields mean. FIBO supplies that meaning. Bind FIBO to Solid and you get semantic interoperability — and we bind an ontology finance already trusts rather than asking anyone to adopt something new. Three use cases: lending on data verified against the borrower's Pod; KYC/AML where identity is proven once and reused by signature check; and bank-provided identity, where a bank issues credentials into the customer's Pod — turning a compliance cost centre into a service line. NatWest has already piloted exactly this. Finance leads because EDMA's roots and FIBO are here, and the cost of the status quo is enormous. Stuart will take it to the bottom line."

Stuart Coleman · business value

Cost out, risk down, new revenue in

Less data custody

Read from the Pod instead of holding copies, lower cost, liability and compliance burden.

Reusable credentials

Proven identity & eligibility reused across sectors, onboarding and KYC/AML cost falls.

Single source of truth

Update once, not everywhere, less reconciliation, fewer stale-copy errors.

Interoperability, no lock-in

Open W3C-track standard; shared sector ontologies, not bespoke integrations.

New permissioned revenue

Value-added services, like bank-provided identity, on user-controlled data.

AI-ready & future-proof

Consented, attributable data for trustworthy AI; aligns with GDPR Art. 20, the EU Data Act & DMA.

ODI × EDM ASSOCIATIONSolid Community of Practice
STUART (~5 min): "I'm Stuart Coleman. Elisa gave the how; here's why it pays, in six ways. One: less data custody — read from the Pod with permission instead of securing your own copy; every copy you don't hold is a breach you can't have. Two: reusable credentials proven once and reused across services and sectors — time and money out of onboarding and KYC/AML. Three: a single current source of truth — the customer updates once, so less reconciliation and fewer stale-data decisions. Four: interoperability without lock-in — an open W3C standard, interoperability from shared ontologies this community defines, not another bespoke integration or a platform you can't leave. Five — cost to growth — new permissioned services and revenue, like bank-provided identity turning a cost centre into a product. Six: future-proofing — structured, consented, attributable data for trustworthy AI and agents, and demonstrable user control that aligns with GDPR Article 20, the EU Data Act and the DMA. Cost out, risk down, new revenue in — while doing right by customers. The way to capture it is to be in the room while the rails are laid."
