## Slide 7 - What Is Solid

**Before (approved)**

> Solid is an open-source **project**, open **standard**, and **movement** for a **user-centric data** storage system, originating from Sir Tim Berners-Lee's lab at MIT CSAIL.
>
> Your data lives in a **Pod**: a personal or organisational datastore with fine-grained access controls. **AI agents and apps** read and write only **with permission**, **breaking down data silos** and keeping data under the control of whoever it describes.
>
> The ODI stewards Solid's standards, codebases, and communities.

**After (aligned)**

> Solid is a privacy-by-design protocol built on standard web technologies.
>
> Rather than each application holding its own copy of a person's or organisation's data, Solid stores it in a **Pod**: a personal or organisational datastore with fine-grained access controls, so **AI agents and apps** read and write only **with permission**.
>
> Because Pods use open, linked-data standards, the same data can be reused across applications without bespoke integration, and it stays under the control of the entity it describes. Solid was developed by Sir Tim Berners-Lee's lab at MIT and their collaborators, and is standardised through the W3C Solid Community Group and the Linked Web Storage Working Group.

**Reason:** Aligns the deck's "What is Solid" slide to the Emma-edited microsite panel while retaining the deck's "AI agents and apps" phrasing.

## Slide 8 - The Problem

**Before (approved)**

> Today, each service collects and stores its own version of the same information. That creates five familiar problems:
>
> - Duplicate records that drift out of date
> - More copies for attackers to target
> - Lock-in when data is tied to one provider
> - Consent that is too broad or hard to change
> - Identity and eligibility checks repeated every time
>
> **Solid addresses this by separating data from applications.** Services read what they are allowed to use from the person's Pod, instead of keeping another copy.

**After (aligned)**

> The same data is collected, copied and secured many times over, yet is rarely current, portable or under the control of the person or organisation it describes. The result:
>
> - Silos and duplication
> - More places for data to be breached
> - Vendor lock-in
> - All-or-nothing consent
> - Having to prove your identity from scratch at every institution
>
> **Solid fixes this by decoupling data from applications.** Services read what they are allowed to use from the person's or organisation's Pod, instead of keeping another copy.

**Reason:** Applies Emma/Grace's plain-language rewrite and restores the microsite phrase "Solid fixes this by decoupling data from applications."
